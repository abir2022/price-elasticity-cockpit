What this answers

If we move price by 1%, how much does volume move, and is a price cut revenue-positive?

How to read it

Below -1 means volume responds more than price, so a cut can grow revenue. Between -1 and 0 volume barely reacts. Positive values usually mean import cost is driving both price and volume, not pricing power. R-squared shows how much of the movement price explains; Level beta is the cross-check.

Source

First-difference regression of monthly ln(volume) on ln(realized price) per SKU, needing 15 or more month-to-month pairs.

Method

  • Realized price = net value ÷ quantity, per selling unit as booked.
  • Month-on-month price compares to the previous month with sales for that SKU.
  • Elasticity = slope of Δln(volume) on Δln(realized price) between consecutive months (first-difference estimator), which strips out the shared cost/season trend. Needs 15+ monthly differences and |β|≤8 to be reported; the level (log-log) estimate is shown alongside.
  • Declared price for a SKU-month resolves in this order: channel price grid (DP = Distributor, TP = Dealer) → retail price log (DP/TP/MRP) → blank. MRP comes only from the retail table.
  • Volume is in each SKU's own selling unit — never add volumes across SKUs.
  • Where realized price ÷ MRP is outside 0.35–1.6 the booked unit does not reconcile to the declared pack; flagged “unit ?”.
